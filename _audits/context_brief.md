## §5 Atom-level vs constant-level distinction (CRITICAL)

The R³ companion paper reports a **102-atom provenance ledger** classifying each atomic operator/algorithm as one of: 47 literature-direct (46%), 24 engine-adapted (24%), 21 literature-composed (20%), 10 novel/invention (10%). The T³ companion reports a **42-atom ledger** at 19/11/8/4 (45/26/19/10%). **These are ATOM-level audits — they classify computations and algorithms, not individual numeric literals.** The current audit is **CONSTANT-LEVEL** — every numeric literal is independently attributed. The two granularities will produce **dramatically different rates**:

- An atom like "Sethares dyad-dissonance kernel" is LIT-DIRECT at atom level (the algorithm is verbatim from Sethares 1993). But its **7 numeric constants** (D*=0.24, S₁=0.0207, S₂=18.96, C₁=5.0, C₂=−5.0, A₁=−3.51, A₂=−5.75) are each independently LIT-VERBATIM at constant level — that maps cleanly.

- An atom like "sensory_pleasantness = `0.6·(1 − sethares_dissonance) + 0.4·stumpf_fusion`" is LIT-COMPOSED at atom level. Its 2 constants (`0.6`, `0.4`) are **ENGINEERING-CHOICE** — Sethares and Stumpf did not jointly publish a 0.6/0.4 mix.

## §8 Audit guidance — what NOT to do

- **Do not infer constant provenance from module docstring inheritance.** A module that opens with "Cites Sethares 1993, Plomp-Levelt 1965" does not transitively make every constant inside LIT-VERBATIM.
